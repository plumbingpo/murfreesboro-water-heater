Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • No hot water at all, even after waiting for the tank to recover
  • Sudden loud knocking or boiling sounds coming from inside the tank
  • Water or steam coming from the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset
  • A puddle under the tank that keeps growing
  • Tap water hot enough to burn, even at the usual setting
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

It is tempting to treat a leaking or dead water heater as a quick fix. In reality, you may be dealing with standing water beside 240-volt wiring, a gas supply and live burner a few inches from a puddle, and a tank of very hot water under pressure. The relief valve, if it is firing, is the last safeguard between an overheated tank and a violent failure, and plugging it is one of the most dangerous things anyone can do to a heater. Relighting a pilot while gas lingers, or cranking on a corroded fitting until it snaps, can make things much worse. Leave, shut off, and call is the right sequence for anyone without the tools and training.

Why call a technician instead of improvising? Certainty and safe sequencing. We verify the shutoffs, test for gas leaks with real instruments, and confirm the area is safe for electrical work. Then we find the root cause. A discharging relief valve often means high street pressure or no room for thermal expansion; a puddle might be a drain valve, a dielectric fitting, condensation, or a failed tank; a cold house may trace back to a thermocouple, an element, or a tripped high-limit switch. No two of those share a fix. We repair what can honestly be repaired, and when the tank is the problem, we explain your replacement options without pressure.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
  • Lift the relief valve lever once a year to confirm it opens and reseats, and call if it keeps dripping
  • Tag the water heater supply valve and the main shutoff so nobody has to search during a leak
