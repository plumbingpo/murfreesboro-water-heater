Most gas water heaters in Murfreesboro, TN work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve shuts off the gas for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Murfreesboro Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight

Two things make gas water heater work a poor DIY project: the parts involve fuel and combustion, and the symptoms overlap. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
