Keeping Your Lebanon Water Heater Running Longer

Most premature tank failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Lebanon

Whatever type of water heater your Lebanon home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units lose heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
