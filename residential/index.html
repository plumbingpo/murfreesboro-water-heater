Clear Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Murfreesboro Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing for every home, covering everything from a failed heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

Most water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Watch for discolored or rusty hot water, a rumbling tank, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or showers that go cold sooner than they used to. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a spent anode rod, or a worn dip tube. An early service call at the first sign usually costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Many homeowners call us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with regular descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your unit has left.

Maintenance Plans

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the idea behind our maintenance plans. Once a year, we drain and flush the tank, measure the anode rod, exercise the relief valve, inspect the gas valve and venting or the elements and wiring, and descale tankless heat exchangers, all tailored to the equipment in your home.

No two maintenance plans look exactly alike. A tank in its later years gets a closer look than a new one, a tankless unit on hard water needs regular descaling to keep its heat exchanger clear, and a large family cycles the tank far harder than a couple does. We set up each visit around your heater, your water, and how your family uses it, so every service keeps the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

The savings add up faster than most people expect. A flushed tank transfers heat with less effort, a replaced anode rod keeps corrosion off the tank walls, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Compared with the cost of replacing a tank early, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Sized for Your Household

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

An undersized water heater runs out halfway through the second shower, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Murfreesboro Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means matching the first-hour rating to your morning rush, rather than simply copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the flow rate in GPM against the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

Needs change over time. A new baby, a teenager, or an in-law suite can outgrow the tank that once kept up just fine. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as you stay within the rated flow, while heat pump water heaters draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. If you're torn between tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Frequent Failures

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

A cold tap where hot water should be, a burner that refuses to light, a rumbling, sediment-heavy tank, a slow drip from the relief valve: every one of these problems has its own cause and needs its own repair. Murfreesboro Water Heater diagnoses the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's an electric tank with a burned-out element, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube mixing cold water into the hot, or a tankless unit throwing error codes.

The right repair depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, it's usually an element or thermostat. On gas units, it tends to be a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes get solved with a thermal expansion tank plus a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before the damage spreads. We also check the temperature setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. Any gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility right away.
