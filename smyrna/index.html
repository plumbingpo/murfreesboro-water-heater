Keeping Your Smyrna Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.
