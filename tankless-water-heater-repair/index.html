Most tankless problems announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. Error codes are a starting point, not a diagnosis, and we treat them that way on every Murfreesboro call. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • Temperature dropping sharply when a second shower or the dishwasher starts
  • Water dripping from the cabinet or the condensate line
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Longer bursts of cold water between hot draws than you used to get

Consider the risks first. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. Electric tankless models are serious electrical loads, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Leave the power on in cold weather, since freeze protection only runs when the unit has electricity
  • Do not keep resetting a recurring code; note it and have it checked
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year so the flow sensor gets full flow
  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Keep the vent termination and air intake clear of leaves, nests, snow, and lint
