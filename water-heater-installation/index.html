Sizing and placement decide most of the outcome. For a tank, the first-hour rating is the number we compare against your household's busiest hour; for a tankless unit, we size by flow rate and winter temperature rise. Location comes next. A gas heater needs a vent route and combustion air, a heat pump heater needs open space and a condensate drain, and a whole-house electric tankless unit usually needs a big dedicated circuit and matching panel capacity. Put a heater above a finished ceiling and it needs a drain pan; put a gas unit in a garage and code may require a raised platform. You get the trade-offs in plain terms, with no pressure.

The final stretch is where a careful installer and a fast one look nothing alike. Every gas joint is leak-tested, vent pitch and clearances follow the manufacturer's spec, the circuit and breaker match the heater, and dielectric fittings separate copper from steel. We pipe the relief valve discharge full size to the right spot, install code-required expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps, and handle the permit if needed. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Morning showers that run cold for the last person
  • An older install without an expansion tank, pan, or straps where required
  • Taps at the far end of the house that take minutes to warm up
  • A property where the water heater has to go in from scratch
  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • Plans to change fuel types or go all-electric
  • A new jetted tub that outpaces the heater's capacity
  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
